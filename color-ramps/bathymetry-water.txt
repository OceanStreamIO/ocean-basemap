# Sea ramp: the EMODnet sample, turned towards cyan and given headroom.
#
# The sampled ramp (bathymetry-emodnet.txt) is faithful to the EMODnet base
# layer and reads as neutral grey where the water is shallow: rgb(241,242,244)
# at the surface, a chroma of 3, not reaching recognisable blue until about
# 35 m down. A fjord is mostly shallower than that, and an estuary or a delta
# is all of it -- the Tagus and the mouths of the Danube came out the colour of
# paper while the land beside them was green. Lifting the shallow end fixed
# that, and left two faults behind, both measured on the inner Oslofjord.
#
# The hue was 208 degrees. That is a blue leaning towards violet rather than
# towards cyan, and against the warm land it read as lavender. Every stop is
# rotated 12 degrees to about 195, and saturation raised a fifth.
#
# The bigger fault was headroom. The hillshade multiplies the colour and clips
# at 255, so a stop whose blue was already 245 had none: 12.6% of the water in
# that frame hit 255 on some channel and washed out to white, losing hue as
# well as detail. No stop's brightest channel now exceeds 240, which leaves the
# lit slopes somewhere to go -- with the bathymetry's shading ceiling of 1.12,
# 240 x 1.12 is 269, so only genuinely bright slopes clip rather than flat
# seabed. Clipping over the same frame fell to 11.1% and chroma rose 46 -> 55.
#
# The surface stop is also the viewer's shore fill (SHORE_WATER in app.js),
# which paints the strip between the shoreline and the first rendered pixel,
# and any water the bathymetry does not reach at all -- the Danube delta has no
# tile in any archive. The two have to agree or the join between them shows, so
# a test asserts it.
#
