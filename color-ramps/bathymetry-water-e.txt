# Sea ramp, candidate E -- a blue shore, clearly.
#
# Chosen on saturation rather than luminance: the published ramp is 2.9%
# saturated at the waterline, which is grey, and darkening grey only gives
# darker grey. Interpolates to the published stop at -87.5 m, so nothing
# below the shelf changes.
#
# Sea ramp: EMODnet's shallows, our deep water.
#
# The shallow end is sampled from the EMODnet World Base Layer over the
# Adriatic and Ionian -- their tiles paired with our depth grid, median colour
# per depth bin. On the shelf they are far paler than we were: at -20 m
# 228 236 244 against our 187 225 240. That is the difference that made the
# Venice lagoon and the northern Adriatic read as two different things on
# their map and as one flat tone on ours.
#
# The 0 m bin from that sampling is discarded. At the very shoreline it is
# contaminated by land and comes back 231 240 234, with green above blue,
# which is not a water colour. The surface stop is extrapolated from -5 and
# -10 instead.
#
# Below -100 m it fades back to the ramp we had, reaching it by -250 m,
# because the two disagree in the other direction down there: EMODnet go
# grey-blue where ours stays cyan, and the cyan deep water was chosen
# deliberately. A cosine fade keeps both joins flat.
#
# The fade alone left the ramp non-monotonic -- EMODnet's curve falls faster
# than ours between -150 and -250, so the map got *lighter* with depth across
# the join. Each stop is clamped against the one above it, on integers, so
# deeper is never lighter by even a rounding unit.
#
# SHORE_WATER in pipeline/xyz.py and app.js must equal the 0 m stop. It fills
# the strip between the OSM shoreline and where the rendered tiles begin, and
# any difference shows as a hairline along every coast.
#
