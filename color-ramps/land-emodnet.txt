# Land ramp: the EMODnet World Base Layer's own colours, sampled over Europe.
#
# Recovered by scripts/sample-emodnet-palette.py -- median base-layer colour
# per elevation bin, which also discards most of the hillshading they bake in.
#
# Sampled over Italy, the Alps and the Dinarides, 2.5 million land pixels.
# That regional choice is the whole point, and it is the third attempt:
#
#   1. Sampled over the whole world. Every bin below 1750 m came back pale and
#      near-neutral, because the median of all land is the median of a lot of
#      dry, bright ground. Shipped, and the uplands looked like desert.
#   2. Variant k cut chroma in 250-1750 m to kill the desert cast. It was the
#      wrong axis: measured against EMODnet over the same ground, what makes
#      their hills read as hills is not less colour but less light. k made the
#      uplands paler still, and Italy came out as one continuous mountain.
#   3. This. Sampled where EMODnet actually is, which is also where the
#      basemap is judged. Lightness falls 219 -> 144 from sea level to 1500 m
#      and chroma rises 5 -> 59, so the colour carries the elevation and the
#      hillshade no longer has to.
#
# The measured difference against k, which is what this replaces:
#
#      elev     EMODnet L / chroma     k L / chroma
#       400 m         188 / 27          222 / 16
#       800 m         172 / 39          210 / 23
#      1500 m         144 / 59          189 / 36
#
# Above 1500 m EMODnet's median stops being a ramp: 2000-3400 m comes back
# near-neutral grey, because that band is Alpine snow and rock rather than a
# tint applied to elevation. It is continued by hand from there -- bare rock,
# then snow -- as it was before.
#
# Paired with a gentler light in pipeline/hypsometric.py. The two go together:
# this ramp under the old hard light is still craggy, and the old ramp under
# this light is flat. Both were rendered before choosing (variants 1-6).
#
