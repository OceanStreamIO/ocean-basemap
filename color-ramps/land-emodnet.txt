# Land ramp: the EMODnet World Base Layer's own colours.
#
# Sampled from their rendered tiles by scripts/sample-emodnet-palette.py --
# median base-layer colour per elevation bin, which also discards most of the
# hillshading they bake in -- and curated from color-ramps/sampled/.
#
# The sampling covers the world's land, not only EMODnet's own extent. Taken
# over Europe alone, every bin below 1750 m was a median of European farmland.
# With desert, sahel, tropics, boreal, steppe and ice in the sample -- 16.7
# million pixels -- those bins barely moved, so the pale, low-chroma lowland
# below is not an artefact of where we looked. It is what EMODnet draws.
#
# Above 1750 m their median stops being a ramp at all: 2250-3250 m comes back
# pure white, because that band is Alpine and Himalayan snow, and 3750 m jumps
# to Tibetan brown. Non-monotonic, because their tint is not a pure function of
# elevation. It is continued by hand from there: bare rock, then snow.
#
# Known trade-off, measured before it was chosen. Against the ramp this
# replaces, chroma falls from 42 to 26 over Morocco and from 35 to 15 over
# Norway. The Atlas and the Sahara gain: warm ochre ridges against a cool pale
# plain, which is the look EMODnet is admired for. Norway loses, because almost
# all of it sits in the near-neutral band below 200 m. EMODnet's own map has
# the same property.
#
# What was ruled out first, so nobody re-runs it: our previous ramp already had
# more chroma, more lightness spread and more hillshade contrast than EMODnet
# on the same ground. Raising any of those, and multidirectional shading, all
# made it worse -- averaging lights cancels them and flattens the terrain. The
# difference is the composition, not the quantity of anything.
#
